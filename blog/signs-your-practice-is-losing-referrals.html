Five signs your practice is losing referrals without noticing

Foram DoshiSeptember 21, 2026 · 5 min read

A referring provider who quietly starts sending patients elsewhere doesn't call to tell you. There's no complaint and no warning — just a slow quarter, months later, that nobody can explain.

Most specialty practices can't answer a simple question: which referring providers have gone quiet this quarter? That isn't carelessness. Referrals arrive by fax, phone, email, and portal, and each one is handled well on its own. The pattern across them is what nobody is watching. Here are five signs that pattern is getting away from you.

01You can't name your top ten referring providers

Not from memory, and not from a report. If the only way to find out who sends you the most patients is for someone to scroll back through months of faxes, you won't notice when number four drops to number fifteen.

02The same office is spelled four different ways

"Oak Street Pediatrics", "Oak St Peds", "OSP", and a doctor's last name typed into the same field. Each entry looks fine on its own. Together they make it impossible to count anything by who sent it — which means you can't see a decline even when the data is technically there.

03Call attempts live in notes, not in fields

A note like 4/8 JC LVM tells the person who wrote it exactly what happened. It tells a report nothing. If your practice has a rule — say, five call attempts before a referral is closed — and the attempts are recorded as free text, the rule can be followed but never checked.

A referral that was never scheduled looks, in most systems, exactly like a referral that never arrived.

04Nobody owns sending the report back

The front desk logs the patient, the physician sees them, billing codes the visit. The consult note going back to the referring provider is everyone's job, which in practice means it depends on who remembers. Slow or missing follow-up is one of the most common reasons referring offices quietly switch specialists.

05Every slow quarter is explained as "seasonal"

Sometimes it is. But if the explanation is always the same and nobody can point to the numbers behind it, "seasonal" is standing in for "we don't know."

A 30-minute check you can do this week
  1. Export the last 12 months of referrals, however they're stored.
  2. Clean up the referring office names so each office appears once.
  3. Count referrals per office for the last 90 days and for the 90 days before that.
  4. Circle every office that dropped by half or more. Those are your conversations to have first.

If step two takes most of the 30 minutes, that's a finding too. It means the information you need exists, but not in a shape anyone can use.
